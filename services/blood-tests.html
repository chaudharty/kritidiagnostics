PATIENT INFORMATION

What Is a Blood Test?

A blood test involves collecting a small sample of blood, usually from a vein in the arm or sometimes from a fingertip. The sample is processed and analysed using methods selected for the specific test. The results provide measurements that a healthcare professional may consider alongside symptoms, medical history and other information.

Different tests examine different components or substances in blood. A blood test is not a diagnosis on its own and cannot detect every condition. Ask the clinician who requested the test what the results may mean for your situation.

Why Are Blood Tests Done?

A qualified healthcare professional may request blood testing for a range of clinical reasons, such as:

Routine health monitoring

To review selected health markers over time.

Checking specific markers

To measure particular substances relevant to a clinical question.

Monitoring existing conditions

To support follow-up as part of a care plan.

Supporting evaluation

To add information to a clinician's assessment.

Preventive screening

When a screening test is considered appropriate.

Follow-up testing

To compare measurements when advised by a clinician.

How to Prepare for a Blood Test

Preparation depends on the specific test. Some tests may require fasting, while others do not. Follow the instructions provided for your test or ask the ordering healthcare professional if you are unsure. Continue or change medication only according to advice from the relevant healthcare professional. Bring previous reports when they are relevant to your appointment.

Tell the collection professional if you have questions about the collection process or feel unwell. If you have a history that may affect collection, discuss it with the care team in advance.
